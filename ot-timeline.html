Dates, sources & king ratings

Dates are approximate BC dates, following a conventional chronology of the monarchy. Stories without a securely known date are positioned illustratively and marked “c.”; co-regencies are simplified. Ezra’s return is shown at the traditional date of 458 BC, and Nehemiah’s at 445 BC. Joel and Obadiah have disputed dates. Prophet tracks indicate approximate activity, not lifespans. “Major” and “minor” refer to book length, not importance. King colors summarize the biblical assessment, with mixed reigns and repentance explained in the notes. A faithful rating does not imply a flawless life.

The later Second Temple period connects Ezra and Nehemiah with the world of the New Testament. “Between the Testaments” is a Christian label, not a claim that Jewish history or writing stopped. Biblical collections differ: 1–2 Maccabees and Sirach are part of Catholic and many Orthodox Old Testaments, but not the Jewish Tanakh or most Protestant Old Testaments. This timeline follows the stories’ settings rather than dating the completion of the Tanakh. Later entries link to their historical or literary sources. Their purple tracks do not carry the good/bad ratings used for the earlier kings. Approximate cultural developments represent periods, and the Gospel narratives overlap the final years shown.
